# Results

How good the search is, how that is known, and what was tried and rejected.

> Generated by `python -m sextant.eval.results_page --write` from the committed
> measurement files. Do not edit it by hand: a test fails when this page and
> the files disagree.

## Where it loses to a plain baseline

On three public test sets (NFCorpus, SciFact and FiQA from BEIR, scored with
the field's standard measure, nDCG@10, beside published figures):

- **Its meaning search loses to the published score of the same meaning model**: NFCorpus 0.312 against 0.316.
- **Its keyword search loses to plain BM25 (the BEIR paper's)** on all three sets: NFCorpus 0.299 against 0.325; SciFact 0.635 against 0.665; FiQA 0.216 against 0.236.
- **Fusing keyword with meaning search lowers the score below meaning search alone**: FiQA 0.341 against 0.372.

The full search, as shipped, beats the paper's BM25 + re-reading baseline on all three sets, so the losses above are in stages the product does not stop at. The tables are [below](#on-public-test-sets).

## How good is it

On 65 test questions over 21 documents, the shipped pipeline puts the right passage first **93%** of the time. Plain meaning search manages 87%. Each stage below is in the product because this table says it earns its place.

| Stage | right passage first | recall@3 | recall@5 | MRR | nDCG@5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning search only | 0.873 | 0.885 | 0.973 | 0.915 | 0.904 |
| Keyword search only | 0.782 | 0.876 | 0.921 | 0.864 | 0.851 |
| Both, fused | 0.909 | 0.921 | 0.973 | 0.950 | 0.931 |
| Both, fused, then re-read (the shipped pipeline) | 0.927 | 0.967 | 0.982 | 0.961 | 0.952 |

Scored on the 55 questions that have an answer. The same four stages on a harder set — 39 questions over one long research paper, 34 with an answer, graded by page:

## On public test sets

Fixed documents and questions, judged by people who never saw this code. Scores are nDCG@10 on each set's test questions, graded, as the BEIR paper reports them:

| Set | documents | questions | meaning | keyword | fused | full search |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NFCorpus | 3,633 | 323 | 0.312 | 0.299 | 0.338 | 0.364 |
| SciFact | 5,183 | 300 | 0.671 | 0.635 | 0.688 | 0.700 |
| FiQA | 57,600 | 648 | 0.372 | 0.216 | 0.341 | 0.375 |

Published, same measure and split:

| Set | plain BM25 | same meaning model | BM25 + re-reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NFCorpus | 0.325 | 0.316 | 0.350 |
| SciFact | 0.665 | 0.645 | 0.688 |
| FiQA | 0.236 | 0.369 | 0.347 |

Two things differ from the published runs: sextant's keyword search is its own index, not the paper's Elasticsearch, and each stage hands on at most 30 passages. Plain BM25 and BM25 + re-reading are from the BEIR paper (Table 2); the meaning model's figure is from the MTEB results. Method and sources: [`public-sets.md`](../learning/public-sets.md).

Reproduce one set:

```bash
python -m sextant.eval.public_sets --set nfcorpus --check
```

That downloads NFCorpus (2.4 MB) from the BEIR host, refuses it unless its checksum is the one BEIR publishes, scores all four stages and fails if any lands more than 0.005 from the table above. About 8 minutes on a MacBook Air; no key, nothing spent.

## Knowing when not to answer

A search that always returns something will happily answer a question the
documents cannot. The last stage's score is what lets the system decline.

- Small set: it declines **8 of 10** questions the documents cannot answer, and wrongly declines 3 of 55 that they can.
- Large set: it declines **5 of 5** questions the documents cannot answer, and wrongly declines 1 of 34 that they can.

## What was tried and rejected (9 ideas)

Each had a pass rule written before it ran. None is argued again without a
new fact; the linked note has the method and the full tables.

| Idea | What the measurement said | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A tree of summaries (RAPTOR) | The questions a tree would serve already scored 1.000 hit@5 without one. | [`summary-chunks.md`](../learning/summary-chunks.md) |
| A newer embedding model (bge-small) | Gains 0.091 first-result hit rate on one test set, loses 0.04 on the other; the rule said both. Re-tested on the public sets: meaning search gains only 0.014 on FiQA against the 0.03 asked, and the full search moves by at most 0.011. | [`embedder-swap.md`](../learning/embedder-swap.md), [`rejected-retest.md`](../learning/rejected-retest.md) |
| Smaller passages (150 tokens instead of 200) | Meaning search alone fell from 0.820 to 0.720 on the handbook set. Re-tested on the public sets: the full search fell 0.005 on NFCorpus where it had to gain 0.03. | [`chunk-size.md`](../learning/chunk-size.md), [`rejected-retest.md`](../learning/rejected-retest.md) |
| Dropping the re-reading stage (the reranker) | On the long-paper set it scores 0.000 on every off-topic question; no other stage separates them. | [`reranker-decision.md`](../learning/reranker-decision.md) |
| Letting the model imagine an answer first (HyDE) | Meaning search fell from 0.873 to 0.800, and after re-reading the score was 0.909 either way. | [`hyde.md`](../learning/hyde.md) |
| Prompt caching | A 28% net saving, against the 30% the rule asked for. | [`prompt-caching.md`](../learning/prompt-caching.md) |
| A fallback below the confidence floor, as a cost saving | Across all 55 answerable questions it fired once. | [`floor-fallback-cost.md`](../learning/floor-fallback-cost.md) |
| The same fallback, as a way to find missed answers | The question it was built for scored 0.5 with it and 0.5 without. | [`floor-fallback.md`](../learning/floor-fallback.md) |
| Choosing a different index per question | One index already reaches 0.996 recall in 1.48 ms at 100k passages. | [`ivfpq-100k.md`](../learning/ivfpq-100k.md) |

## What a question costs

**About ₹0.13 a question** ($0.0015): 110 complete answers cost $0.164 in the whole-set run that note records, converted at the note's own rate of ₹84 to the dollar ([`floor-fallback-cost.md`](../learning/floor-fallback-cost.md)). Searching alone costs nothing: the search models run on your machine, and only writing the answer calls a paid model.

## How fast it is on a big library

Measured on a library of 85,362 passages (the public FiQA set) on a MacBook Air M2, 8 GB, over 200 of its questions:

| | typical | slowest 1 in 20 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full search, as shipped | 582 ms | 650 ms |
| Meaning search alone | 15 ms | 25 ms |
| Keyword search alone | 67 ms | 100 ms |

The first answer after opening the library takes **8.7 s** with the search models already downloaded (they load, and the keyword index is built). The process peaks at **1.0 GB** of memory, and the index takes 521 MB of disk. Most of a search is the final reranking step, whose cost does not grow with the library.

This is **not slow** by the rule set before measuring (slow would be the slowest 1 in 20 full searches over 2 s or meaning searches over 200 ms, or a first answer over 30 s), so faster index types stay parked.

## Check it yourself

```bash
sextant-eval
```

That rebuilds the index from the 21 committed documents and prints the first table above. It needs no key and spends nothing. `sextant-eval --check` compares the run against the committed figures and fails on any drop over 0.02; that check runs on every change to the project.

What these numbers do **not** show: the first two tables' questions and
documents were written together, and the public sets are three particular
collections, so neither says how the search will do on someone else's library.
